## The Setup, Step by Step

**Step 1: Create a dedicated account for your ClawdBot.**

This is a security and isolation move. Don't wire your bot directly into your main X presence. Give the bot its own account so its actions never touch your main identity, and if anything goes sideways, the blast radius is contained.

**Step 2: Auth with your X account.**

Log into x.com in Chrome or Brave. Then grab two cookies from the browser: `auth-token` and `ct0`.

You'll find them in DevTools (F12) → Application tab → Cookies → x.com. Copy both values and provide them to OpenClaw so it can access your account.

Treat those cookies like passwords. They're session credentials. Don't paste them into public repos, screenshots, or group chats.

**Step 3: Point it at a real account and start asking.**

Ask OpenClaw to take a look at the account, fetch the last N tweets, and fire away with any questions you like.

```
Fetch the last 50 tweets from @handle.
Which topics got the most engagement?
What do the top posts have in common?
```

That's it. No dashboard to configure, no data connector to debug.

## Prompts That Pull Real Answers

The generic "analyze this account" prompt gets you a generic summary. Ask like you'd ask a sharp analyst who's been staring at the profile all day.

**For growth hackers:**

```
Compare the last 100 tweets from @competitor with my last 100.
Which topics do they win on that I don't touch?
What formats do their top 10 posts share?
```

**For recruiters:**

```
Fetch @candidate's last 30 tweets.
Summarize their communication style and technical depth.
Quote 3 posts that show how they handle disagreement.
```

**For researchers:**

```
Pull the last 200 tweets from @handle.
Cluster them by topic. Show the distribution and the tone of each cluster.
```

The follow-ups are where this setup beats every report-style tool. When the bot says "tech commentary drives your best engagement," you can ask *which* posts, *why* those, and *what to try next* — until the answer actually lands.
