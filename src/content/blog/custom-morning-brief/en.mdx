# My Morning Brief Now Writes Itself (and It's Actually Useful)

I used to start every day the same way: open laptop, scan 8 tabs, scroll three newsletters, check my calendar, stare at my to-do list, and try to figure out what actually mattered.

Thirty minutes gone. Nothing done.

Now I wake up to a single message on Telegram. It has my tasks for the day, relevant news I'd miss otherwise, a full draft for whatever content I'm making, and a list of things my AI agent can handle while I drink coffee.

One message replaces the eight-app morning scramble.

## The real problem with mornings

Here's what nobody talks about: your morning isn't wasted because you're lazy. It's wasted because you're orienting.

You're not doing work. You're figuring out *what* work to do. That takes cognitive load you haven't built up yet.

Meanwhile, your AI agent sat idle for eight hours.

The morning brief fixes this by turning those overnight hours into prep time. You wake up to work already organized, research already done, drafts already written.

## What's actually in the brief

I set mine up through OpenClaw. Every day at 8:00 AM, it sends me a structured report with four sections:

**1. Relevant news** — not generic headlines. I told it I care about AI, startups, and tech. It browses the web overnight and surfaces what matters.

**2. Content drafts** — this is the part that surprised me. It doesn't suggest ideas. It writes full drafts. Scripts, email drafts, outlines. Wake up to a draft, not a bullet point.

**3. Today's tasks** — pulls directly from my to-do list. No switching apps to see what's due.

**4. AI-recommended tasks** — this is the most underrated part. The agent proactively suggests things it can do for me today. Not waiting for instructions. Thinking ahead.

That last section is what makes this feel different from a glorified RSS feed.

## The setup (it's simpler than you'd think)

First, connect OpenClaw to your messaging platform and your task manager. It works with Telegram, Discord, and iMessage on the messaging side. For tasks, it integrates with Todoist, Apple Reminders, and Asana.

Then send this prompt:

```text
I want to set up a regular morning brief. Every morning at 8:00 AM,
send me a report through Telegram.

I want this report to include:
1. News stories relevant to my interests (AI, startups, tech)
2. Ideas for content I can create today
3. Tasks I need to complete today (pull from my to-do list)
4. Recommendations for tasks you can complete for me today

For the content ideas, write full draft scripts/outlines — not just titles.
```

That's it. OpenClaw schedules it automatically.

Check your messages the next morning to verify it's working.

## Tweaking without touching a config file

Here's the part I actually use the most: you customize by just texting your bot.

```text
Add weather forecast to my morning brief.
```

```text
Stop including general news, focus only on AI.
```

```text
Include a motivational quote each morning.
```

No YAML editing. No re-deploying. Just text.

And if you're not sure what to include? Send this:

```text
I want this report to include things relevant to me.
Think of what would be most helpful to put in this report.
```

Let the agent figure out what you need. Sounds lazy, but it works surprisingly well.

## Why full drafts matter

Early on, I almost stopped including the content section because "idea suggestions" felt useless. A list of titles doesn't save me time — I can generate titles all day.

The key is in the prompt: *write full draft scripts/outlines — not just titles.*

Now I wake up to a 400-word outline I can actually edit. Or a script draft I can record. That's real time saved.

If your morning brief just gives you bullet points, you're doing it wrong.

## The AI-recommended tasks section changed how I use agents

Most people use AI reactively. You have a task, you ask it to help.

The morning brief flips that. Every day, my agent tells me:

"Here are three things I can do for you today."

Maybe it's summarizing a long thread I flagged. Maybe it's drafting a reply to a customer email. Maybe it's researching a competitor's latest launch.

I don't have to think of what to delegate. The agent suggests it. I just say yes or no.

This one section probably saves me an hour per day of "what should I hand off?" deliberation.

## This works regardless of your industry

The guide mentions this explicitly, and I agree: it doesn't matter what you do.

Founder? Brief covers your competitors, your task list, your content calendar.

Executive? Brief covers your industry, your direct reports' projects, your meeting prep.

Knowledge worker? Brief covers your niche, your deadlines, your research backlog.

Tasks, news, and proactive suggestions are universally useful. The specifics change; the structure doesn't.

## What I'd do differently starting out

I underestimated how much I'd iterate. My first brief was fine. My current brief is four times better because I kept texting small tweaks.

"Make the news section shorter."
"Move tasks to the top."
"Don't include tasks due next week, only today."

Treat the first version as a starting point, not a finished product.
